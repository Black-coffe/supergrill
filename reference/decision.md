# Lens: decision — personal & business choices, hiring, buy-vs-build, life

Goal: the user makes the decision with eyes open — options seen, values named, biases surfaced. You never make the decision for them.

Tone note: for personal or emotional topics default to **soft-medium**; surface distortions as curious questions, never as diagnoses. Default level: `quick` for two-way doors, `deep` for one-way doors.

## Question tracks

1. **The actual decision** — what exactly is being decided, by when, and what happens if no decision is made (the default option is always an option)?
2. **Options space** — list ALL live options including the uncomfortable ones and "do nothing". Any option the user refuses to even name gets one respectful question: what makes it unthinkable?
3. **Criteria & weights** — what matters, in what order? Force ranking: "if you could only have one of these two, which?"
4. **Reversibility** — one-way or two-way door? Two-way doors deserve speed, not more analysis. One-way doors deserve the full grill.
5. **Evidence per option** — what do you actually KNOW about each option vs assume? What's the cheapest experiment that would change the ranking?
6. **Bias sweep** (as questions):
   - Sunk cost: "if you were starting today from zero, same choice?"
   - Anchoring: "forget the first number you heard — from scratch, what's it worth?"
   - Optimism: "your estimate assumes nothing goes wrong. What's the estimate with normal-life friction?"
   - Social proof: "whose approval are you optimising for in this option?"
   - Status quo: "inaction has a price too — what is it here, per month?"
7. **Time-horizon check** — 10 minutes / 10 months / 10 years from now: how does each option feel? Which choice would the 80-year-old you respect?
8. **Regret minimisation & pre-mortem** — for the leading option: it's a year later and you regret it. What happened?

## Super-level document checklist

- Written options with costs and dates (offers, quotes, contracts, job descriptions)
- Budget or cash-flow view for the period the decision affects
- Any prior attempt at the same decision and what happened
- Constraints from third parties (partner, board, lease, visa, contract terms)
- Calendar or timeline constraints

Suggested stages for `super`: (1) the decision, options, criteria; (2) evidence per option, requires the written options; (3) biases, horizons, pre-mortem; (4) decision and review date.

## Output

Brief = decision memo: options table (criteria × options, with the user's own weights), reversibility class, evidence vs assumption per option, bias flags raised, the user's decision (or the named experiment that precedes it) and the review date.
